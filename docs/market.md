# Market and competition

[Documentation](README.md) · [Product scope](product.md) · [Regulatory pathway](regulatory-pathway.md)

Research checked 20 October 2026. This is a working note for further market
research, not an accepted implementation plan, pricing decision or finding that
Ledova is ready for real-world use. The product and regulatory documents linked
above retain those boundaries.

## Current assessment

Equity administration is an established market with evidence of customer demand
and growth among some providers. Public evidence does not establish Cake as the
market leader or a broad boom in Australian private-company registry software.
Whether customers will choose and pay for Ledova remains untested.

case study is not a representative customer survey or a quantified saving.

## Hypotheses to test for Ledova

- Australian private companies with several shareholders, recurring ownership
  changes or an active employee scheme may benefit more than founder-only
  companies with infrequent changes. This is a proposed customer segment, not a
  validated market.
- The value may be less administrative work, fewer mistakes and clearer
  ownership and employee entitlements. Simplicity must be demonstrated through
  those outcomes; competitors already market ease of use.
- A mirror can create duplicate maintenance alongside the company's legal
  register. Establish who keeps both aligned and whether Ledova saves more work
  than it introduces. The company's existing legal arrangements remain in place.
- Accurate records, trust, onboarding effort, useful exports and adviser
  involvement may determine adoption as much as interface design or price.

These hypotheses do not add features to the [roadmap](roadmap.md). Competitors'
trading, payments, filings or advanced equity features are not reasons to expand
Ledova's accepted scope.

## Next research

1. Interview five to eight company administrators and two or three accountants
   or company secretaries. Ask about their last actual share issue or employee
   grant, the work and cost involved, errors, existing tools and switching
   barriers. Do not treat hypothetical interest as a purchase commitment.
2. Compare the same three journeys in Ledova, Cake and Orchestra: record an
   existing register, record an investor issue and manage an employee
   entitlement. Use synthetic examples and record effort, confusion and gaps.
3. Test willingness to pay and seek three concrete pilot commitments. Establish
   the onboarding work, expected price, reason to switch and evidence of repeat
   use. Any real-world pilot remains subject to the existing regulatory and
   release boundaries.
4. Revisit the customer segment and proposition using those findings before
   proposing more functionality. No customer interviews, commitments or paid
   pilots are established by this note.

Update this page when evidence changes, retaining the source, its period and the
date checked. Record findings here; accepted product changes still belong in
GitHub issues and the existing product and decision documents.
