## Contracts

The contracts in `contracts/contracts/` are Solidity, OpenZeppelin-based and owned by the operator
key: the factory makes each class and its company's registry owned by the address the backend signs
with.

| Contract                | Responsibility |
| ----------------------- | -------------- |
| `WhitelistRegistry.sol` | One company's allowlist. `setExpiry(address, uint64)` is the only write and is owner-only; `expiresAt(address)` is the stored expiry; `isWhitelisted(address)` is `expiresAt > block.timestamp`. Zero removes a wallet and `type(uint64).max` never expires. It has no pause: the token pause is the incident lever |
| `ShareToken.sol`        | One share class. ERC-20 with 0 decimals, burnable, pausable, bound at construction to its company's registry (`whitelist()`). `authorizedShares` is the cap; `mint` reverts with `ExceedsAuthorizedShares` unless the cap holds; `_update` refuses any movement to a wallet the registry does not list and any transfer from one, so an expired or removed holder can neither receive nor send, directly or through `transferFrom`. Burning is not checked. `setAuthorizedShares` cannot go below `totalSupply()` and emits `AuthorizedSharesUpdated(oldAmount, newAmount)` |
| `ShareTokenFactory.sol` | `createShareToken(name, symbol, identifier, acn, authorizedShares, owner)`, `getTokenByIdentifier(identifier)` and `registryOf(acn)`. The identifier is the deduplication key; the factory refuses one that does not begin with the company's `<acn>:`, and the backend always sends `<acn>:<symbol>`. A company's first class creates its `WhitelistRegistry`, owned by the class owner, and emits `WhitelistRegistryCreated(acn, registry)`; its later classes share that registry and must have the same owner. Each class emits `ShareTokenCreated(token, identifier, symbol, authorizedShares)` |
| `AtomicSwap.sol`        | EIP-712 swap settlement (domain `LedovaAtomicSwap`, version 1) between an approved share token and an approved payment token, executed by an authorised relayer. It holds no registry: the share token's own `_update` checks both parties |
| `AUDY.sol`              | Minter-gated AUD stablecoin, 2 decimals, the payment token. Its `assets.Asset` row plus its `AssetChainDeployment` on the operator's receiving chain are the only representation of a settlement token; `backend/operators/settlement.py` resolves them |
| `AUSG.sol`              | NAV-bearing token, 6 decimals, with a redemption queue and `NAVUpdated` events; not part of the issuance flow |

The deployment scripts, the addresses they write and the chain ids they accept are in
[chain setup](../operations/chains.md#allowed-chains-and-finality).

### Compiler and toolchain pin

The compiler settings are a contract, not a default: Solidity 0.8.24 with `evmVersion: "paris"`, the
optimizer at 200 runs and `viaIR` on (`contracts/hardhat.config.ts`), and OpenZeppelin pinned at
5.4.0 (`contracts/package.json`). Any change to those four moves the generated bytecode of every
contract, so a dependency update that needs one of them is not a dependency update: it needs a
deliberate bytecode and toolchain review. Why the pin stays, what would reopen it, and why the
contracts package's advisories need their own reading are in the
[contracts toolchain decision](../decisions.md#contracts-compiler-and-toolchain). Compilation and
the manual chain check are in [testing](testing.md).

### What the contracts allow and refuse

These are properties of the deployed bytecode, established by the bypass review recorded in
[approval controls](../operations/approval-controls.md#the-bypass-review). They hold whatever the
backend does.

- `_update` is the single enforcement point. A direct `transfer`, a delegated `transferFrom` and a
  swap settlement all meet it, so each is refused when either party is expired or removed, and a
  mint to a recipient with no approval is refused.
- Expiry is exclusive. A movement in the last second an approval names is allowed; in the second the
  expiry names, a transfer, mint or swap settlement is refused.
- Burns are not checked, because a burn has no recipient for `_update` to check. A holder may burn
  their own shares, by the owner's decision, and register reconciliation reports it. `burnFrom` by a
  spender the holder approved succeeds even against an expired or removed holder, since the holder
  granted that allowance while approved; the same spender's `transferFrom` of those shares is
  refused, and `burnFrom` without an allowance is refused.
- A swap settlement with a seller never approved or expired is refused with both nonces preserved; a
  removed seller or buyer is refused with the nonce preserved; an expired buyer is refused and no
  payment moves.
- The factory keys one registry per ACN. Two classes of one company share it: one approval covers
  both and one removal freezes both. An approval in one company's registry grants nothing on another
  company's token, directly or through a swap.
- Only the registry's owner can call `setExpiry` (the factory's owner cannot), and only the token's
  owner can pause. A paused token refuses transfer, mint, delegated transfer and burn, and refuses
  swap settlement until it is unpaused.
- `renounceOwnership` on a registry freezes it for good: no approval can be granted or renewed, so
  every approval can only lapse and every holding freezes with it. On a token it freezes minting,
  cap changes and the pause lever for good, while the registry still governs transfers.
- A token the factory did not create, once the swap's owner approves it, moves through a swap with no
  approval check, because the swap relies on the token's own `_update`; a `ShareToken` deployed
  outside the factory still enforces the registry it was given. The backend never approves such a
  token: a swap approval is built from a `TokenDeployment`, and a deployment whose factory is not the
  configured one is never projected. That is a backend rule, not a contract one.

### Why splits and consolidations cannot run on a live class

No model, service, endpoint, register entry kind or contract function for a split or consolidation
exists; [issuing shares](../business/issuing-shares.md#splits-and-consolidations) says
what each would be
and the [decision](../decisions.md#splits-and-consolidations) why the work is deferred. This is
what the contracts and the stored register allow today.

Shares are whole units at every layer, enforced rather than assumed:

| Layer          | What fixes whole units |
| -------------- | ---------------------- |
| Contract       | [`ShareToken.decimals()`](../../contracts/contracts/ShareToken.sol) is `pure` and returns `0` |
| Share class    | `decimals` has a zero-only validator and the `share_token_whole_units` check constraint (`backend/tokens/models/share_token.py`) |
| Register       | `RegisterPosition.shares` and `ShareRegister.issued_supply` are `DecimalField(decimal_places=0)` (`backend/tokens/models/register.py`) |
| Register event | The register entry guard (`tokens_guard_register_entry` in `backend/tokens/database_guards.sql`) requires every change's `shares` to be a whole, non-zero, signed integer |

So there is no fractional share to round into and no cash rail to pay a fraction out with, and a
corporate action that produces a remainder cannot be recorded. `RegisterInstructionKind` has `issue`
and `transfer` only. Of the entry kinds, a `cessation` must take a member to exactly zero and a
`correction` must be the exact inverse of an earlier entry. A `mirror_correction` does record signed
differences, but only against an imported register's applied snapshot, before deployment and before
any entry other than the opening and earlier mirror corrections, under
`backend/tokens/database_mirror_guards.sql`. No kind can reduce a holding partially on a live class.

[`ShareToken.sol`](../../contracts/contracts/ShareToken.sol) is the whole of what a deployed class can do:

| Member                                     | Effect |
| ------------------------------------------ | ------ |
| `authorizedShares`                         | The cap, readable by anyone |
| `mint(address, uint256)`                   | `onlyOwner`, `whenNotPaused`, reverts `ExceedsAuthorizedShares` when `totalSupply() + amount > authorizedShares` |
| `setAuthorizedShares(uint256)`             | `onlyOwner`, refuses any amount below `totalSupply()` |
| `pause` / `unpause`                        | `onlyOwner`; the pause reaches every movement through `ERC20Pausable._update` |
| `burn` / `burnFrom` (from `ERC20Burnable`) | Holder-side: `burn` destroys the caller's own shares, `burnFrom` spends an allowance the holder granted |
| `_update`                                  | Refuses a recipient the company registry does not list, and a sender it does not list, on every movement |

There is no rebase, balance scaling factor, owner-initiated burn, snapshot extension or supply-wide
write, and the factory deploys with `new ShareToken(...)` behind no proxy, so a deployed class is its
bytecode permanently. A split is therefore executable today only as one issuance per holder,
sequenced after a capital increase that lifts the cap to at least the post-split supply: `mint` and
the issuance preflight both refuse beyond the cap, so an unsequenced split stops part-way with the
register and the chain still agreeing. A consolidation is not executable at all. Nothing can reduce a
holder's shares: `ERC20Burnable` gives only the holder-side `burn` and `burnFrom`, `ERC20Pausable`
blocks both while paused, the platform holds no investor keys, and the only holder-authorised chain
action it relays is an EIP-712 swap settled by `AtomicSwap`, which moves shares and destroys none.
Until a class can destroy shares under company authority, an entry kind can reduce a holding without
ending the membership, and there is an answer for the holder who will not cooperate, a consolidation
is refused rather than attempted.

## Issuance data flow

1. The company owner creates a `ShareToken` in `DRAFT` through `POST /api/v1/tokens/` with a name,
   symbol and `total_supply` (the authorised cap). The historical API key `totalSupply` is this same
   cap; `issuedSupply` and the contract's `totalSupply()` are shares actually issued. Shares use
   whole units: model validation and the `share_token_whole_units` constraint require
   `ShareToken.decimals` to be zero, matching the contract; settlement assets keep their own
   decimals. Through the API a new symbol is 1 to 5 ASCII letters, stored in capitals. Neither the
   API nor admin accepts a new or changed symbol that a supported asset uses (`SUPPORTED_ASSETS` in
   `backend/assets/services/sync.py`: BTC, ETH, USDC, USDT, AUDY, AUSG), in any letter case; existing
   classes keep their symbols. A company may have several classes.
2. A company deployment proposal admits one original deployment job
   ([share-class deployment](#share-class-deployment)).
3. The worker signs `createShareToken` through the outgoing journal; deployment mints nothing and
   `totalSupply()` starts at zero
   ([outgoing signing foundation](#outgoing-signing-foundation)).
4. Projection records the attributed contract and queues the
   [automatic swap approval](#automatic-swap-approval).
5. A participant nominates a proven wallet and the company approves its registry expiry
   ([whitelist changes](#whitelist-changes)).
6. A company applies a non-paid chain grant or a paid issue
   ([share issuances](#share-issuances)).
7. The worker mints, waits for the network finality policy, completes the issuance and records the
   register ISSUE ([share issuances](#share-issuances)).
8. A [capital increase](#capital-increases) raises the cap and mints
   nothing.
9. [Pause and unpause](#pause-and-unpause) stop and resume every movement.
10. Deployment writes a verified `assets.Asset` (`tokenized_security`, `decimals` 0) and an
    `AssetChainDeployment` at the address the factory attests through `getTokenByIdentifier`
    (`bridge_share_asset` in `backend/tokens/services/share_token_service.py`); completing an
    issuance writes the recipient's `Holding` from `balanceOf`. Classes deployed before the bridge
    existed are bridged by `manage.py bridge_share_assets`, which is idempotent and never runs on its
    own.

An import-origin register never uses steps 6 and 7, even after its zero book is deployed: its issues
and transfers are non-paid ledger entries, including grants linked to employee awards and external
allotments, and a later tokenisation must mirror holdings without issuing them twice
([issuing shares](../business/issuing-shares.md#grants-to-walletless-members)).

### Request notes and reads

Reviewers write `review_notes`; execution attempts append timestamped entries to `execution_notes`
in the database, so an older request object cannot overwrite an intervening attempt. Successful
execution adds its outcome to that same log after earlier refusals
(`ReviewableRequest.mark_executed` in `backend/tokens/models/review_request.py`), so the history
does not stop at the last failure. Reviewer notes are omitted from the company-facing serializers and
from the generated `ShareIssuanceRequest` client type; the company receives execution notes and
rejection or supersession reasons.

A company reads its issuance requests through `GET /api/v1/tokens/issuance-requests/`, a read-only
list filtered by `token`, `company` or `status`. It keeps the current company-owner check even where
the database lets a subscriber read the linked request for withdrawal checks. Retained private
capital requests are listed by `GET /api/v1/tokens/capital-increases/` under the same owner scope.
Preparing or approving a request writes database state without opening a chain connection; chain
checks belong to execution.

## Outgoing signing foundation

`backend/blockchain/services/outgoing.py` is the one journal through which the operator key
(`BLOCKCHAIN_OPERATOR_KEY`) signs and sends. Share-class deployment and its automatic swap approval,
whitelist changes, capital increases, share issuances, pause and unpause, settlement-asset and
yield-token mints, NAV updates and swap execution all use it. Participant-signed swap approvals are
the one settlement writer outside it, because the backend never holds the participant's key: their
delivery is journaled and replayed as exact bytes by `tokens.SwapApprovalSubmission`
([participant approvals](trading-protocols.md#participant-approvals)). Wallet
transfers signed by users have their own journals
([wallet protocols](wallet-protocols.md)) and admit no operator signer.

### Signer authority and uncertain replies

Company appointees decide deployments, wallet instructions, grants, paid issues, capital increases
and pauses through the [company workflow API](backend.md#company-workflow-api);
the signer and the PostgreSQL operator role execute bounded accepted instructions and confer no
company appointment
([company authority boundaries](README.md#company-authority-boundaries)).
Settlement-asset and yield-token minting, NAV updates, signer admission and infrastructure recovery
are platform-staff work, not delegated to company administrators.

An uncertain preparation or decision reply keeps its exact original body and key. Current read
access recovers the retained receipt, including after the step's appointment has expired or the
proposal has changed stage; a changed retry conflicts, and no replay after process loss is promised.
Clients scope saved request state to the current account and session.

### Locks, holds and recovery

Every family takes its source and class locks before the outgoing operation and signer locks, and
makes chain calls outside those locks. New unsigned work rechecks the current company, capability,
mandate and exact terms immediately before the local signature. Temporary contention or loss of an
unsigned source holds the original unsigned claim with no signature, attempt or nonce. Only explicit
revocation or database-clock expiry of the consumed approval can fail a genuinely never-signed
claim, and it does not recategorise a definite preparation failure or signed work. Once signed, work
recovers its original bytes, nonce and receipt under operator authority after any later loss of the
human source; revocation never permits discarding or replacing a signed transaction, which keeps its
recorded identity. None of these projections is taken over by the generic transaction monitor.

### Signer admission

Signing needs explicit admission. Existing and new `SigningAccount` rows start `closed`, and a
missing row is also closed. A nonce counter, a successful legacy status or an inventory capture never
grants admission. The [fresh Base Sepolia bootstrap](../operations/chains.md#fresh-signer-admission)
(`bootstrap_fresh_signer`) is the only first-admission command for a public testnet, and
[local chain admission](#local-chain-admission) the only one for the local
development chain. Neither can reopen a signer or authorise legacy cutover, and there is no admin
edit surface. On a public network the signer stays closed until the owner explicitly authorises
signer activation and the bootstrap runs in a fresh environment; company self-service does not relax
the bootstrap, same-key writer drain or cutover requirements. Admitted synthetic test fixtures
establish a test precondition only.

`close_signer_admission(chain_id=..., sender=...)` is an operator-only service that closes an
account and advances its admission generation. It preserves claims, outcomes, counters and every
signed payload; missing accounts are created closed. Closure blocks preparation RPC, new signing and
exact-byte broadcast; receipt reconciliation remains available. Preparation records the generation,
and signing checks it again under the operation and signer locks, so a delayed preparer cannot
survive a close and reopen. The maximum signed 64-bit generation is reserved for closure, so an
admitted account can always close; further advancement at that maximum is refused without wrap or
reset.

Broadcast admission takes a short operation-then-signer lock and commits before RPC. A call that
crossed this boundary before closure may still send and record its result afterwards: closure is a
drain barrier, not instant cancellation or credential revocation, and already signed reservations
and exact-byte recovery must survive that interval.

Before a later adapter is activated, every old same-key process and signing tool must drain and lose
credential access. Recapture history after that drain, bind trusted authorisation and intent, and
import reservations or quarantine unresolved signers. Every remaining same-key writer must use the
foundation or be disabled without legacy fallback; old binaries do not consult this admission guard,
and the standalone Hardhat deployment scripts send outside it. Provider absence, terminal history or
today's key and chain cannot establish historical authorisation or release a nonce. App-role handoff
and each adapter's durable transaction boundary still require proof. After signed activity, rollback
cannot restore legacy sending with that key. Admission and the
[legacy inventory](../operations/chains.md#legacy-inventory) do not establish a
global nonce guarantee or resolve old business operations.

### Local chain admission

`admit_local_signer` admits the configured `BLOCKCHAIN_OPERATOR_KEY` address on the local
development chain, chain id 31337, and on no other chain. The local Compose stack runs it as the last
step of its `migrate` service, after `chain-deploy` has verified the core contracts
([the local chain](../operations/chains.md#the-local-stacks-chain)). Outside any database
transaction it reads what the database recorded for the signer first and the provider's chain id and
mined nonce after, so a transaction mined and recorded between the two reads cannot look missing. It
then admits under the signer lock with the same boundary as the other entry points: an operator
connection, autocommit and no enclosing transaction block.

The configured chain id must be the integer 31337, the operator key must be valid, and the provider
must answer 31337; otherwise nothing is admitted. A missing signer, or a closed one never admitted
(generation zero), enters generation one with its nonce counter unchanged; an admitted signer is
returned unchanged, so the command is idempotent. A signer closed after admission stays closed: as
with the fresh bootstrap, this command cannot undo a closure. Every run also refuses a chain that no
longer holds what the database recorded: each nonce from the chain's mined count up to the signer's
next nonce must belong to a signed transaction still awaiting its receipt. Otherwise the chain was
reset, or lost its latest blocks, without the database, and signing would leave a nonce gap the node
never mines past. The output is one JSON object naming the chain, the address, the generation and
whether anything changed.

### Preparing, signing and broadcasting

Callers provide a stable operation key and immutable intent: chain, sender, target, value and
calldata. Reusing a key with different terms is refused. `prepare_operation` reads the endpoint,
pending nonce, gas price and gas estimate outside database transactions. `sign_operation` then locks
the operation followed by the chain-and-sender account, allocates a nonce from the greater of the
observed pending nonce and the durable counter, and signs locally without RPC. The signed bytes,
fixed hash, nonce reservation and operation pointer commit together before `broadcast_operation`
can submit anything. Every entry point refuses an enclosing database transaction or disabled
autocommit.

Signing an already prepared claim returns the winning attempt once another worker has signed,
provided the signer remains admitted at the same generation. Restarting an unsigned failed attempt
changes its claim identifier and fences out delayed workers. Once signed, uncertainty never
authorises another nonce: retries validate and broadcast the saved bytes, and missing receipts,
provider errors, already-known responses and nonce errors leave the operation unresolved.

The API signs EIP-155 legacy gas-price transactions, including contract creation. Chain ids and gas
limits fit a positive signed 64-bit database integer; allocated nonces stop one below its maximum so
the next counter still fits. Transaction value and gas price accept unsigned 256-bit values.

### Receipts and outcomes

Receipt updates require the same claim and hash. Before a success or revert becomes terminal, the
receipt must name a nonzero block hash that matches the canonical block read by its number. The
service rereads the receipt and that block, and checks the endpoint's chain directly before and after
those reads. Missing, provisional, changed or noncanonical evidence leaves the original signed attempt
unresolved; Base's provisional all-zero block hash is one such case
([recovery](../operations/recovery.md)). All these RPC reads finish before the recording
transaction, whose claim and attempt fences are checked again under the lock.

A recorded revert permits a new claim and nonce while retaining the immutable earlier attempt.
Individual adapters may refuse restart: swap execution keeps a single original claim even after a
revert. `confirmed` means a successful receipt with canonical inclusion was observed; the foundation
applies no confirmation depth, replacement detection or reorg repair. Share issuances, swap
settlement and chain register openings additionally wait for the configured network finality policy
([wallet chain observations](wallet-protocols.md#wallet-chain-observations));
the other families complete on the canonical receipt and their own event checks.

### Journal storage and guards

PostgreSQL enforces immutable attempts, monotonic signer counters and guarded operation transitions.
The `OutgoingOperation`, `SignedAttempt` and `SigningAccount` tables deny application-role access,
even with a user principal; operator access is required. They have no admin or serializer surface.
The [company pack](records.md#chain-evidence) carries each operation's
intent, status and receipt and each attempt's hash, nonce, signer and chain id, never the signed
payload. Signed payloads are broadcast capabilities and belong in protected backups; errors retain a
category rather than provider or database exception text.

The guards for every family are installed on a fresh database by `shared/0002_database_guards` from
each app's `database_guards.sql`. A row without a dispatch UUID or company source is historical work
needing operator attribution, with no automatic retry or legacy sender fallback; the owner confirmed
that ledova.io holds none ([decision](../decisions.md#backend-migration-baseline)).

### Share-class deployment

What the company does and the register states that refuse a deployment are in
[issuing shares](../business/issuing-shares.md#deploying-an-empty-class).

`/api/v1/tokens/register-deployments/` offers create, list, detail, `decision-preview` and `decide`.
Creation takes `operation_id`, `appointment` and `token`; there is no upload, wallet picker or
separate receipt or restart route. Responses provide a typed review snapshot, intent digest, company
decision history, the original deployment association and execution requirements or receipt. A draft
requires an active company, the company's operator wallet or a verified owner wallet on Base (the
captured `issuer` address, contract metadata separate from the technical sender and gas payer) and
proven empty register state. Preparation freezes the company, class and issuer metadata, the factory
intent and the register boundary. The proposal identifier is the original preparation operation
identifier, separate from the deployment, outgoing operation, claim and transaction identifiers; the
retained consumed approval identifies the decision used by application. The public review snapshot
holds company, class, register and transaction facts and the selected address; the captured wallet,
account, profile and user identifiers stay private. Current register-read capability controls
proposal reads; class metadata access grants no access to private legacy owner histories.

Application consumes the exact approval; the original deployment UUID, `DEPLOYING` status and the
actual applying principal's job commit together, and an exact replay returns the retained receipt
without a new effect. An admitted deployment fences register head advances, including imports and
corrections, until its projection completes. There is no fresh owner or staff deploy admission.

`backend/tokens/services/deployment.py` materialises one immutable private `TokenDeployment` for each
admitted job, under the original applied source and intent, including when source loss now holds
unsigned work. Its UUID snapshots preserve token, company and issuer identity; new journals also
retain their exact company proposal and consumed approval, and captured wallet, account, profile and
user identifiers remain private JSON associations rather than customer-deletion foreign keys. Its
immutable nullable source association preserves legacy journals without inventing company approval:
historical rows with null submission identities keep their original hashes and records, are not
imported into the outgoing journal, and cannot obtain a fresh signature. Every fresh signature
rechecks the original mandates, captured wallet association, configuration, terms and register
state; the original selected address is checked rather than replaced by a newer wallet.

The signing callback commits the `SignedAttempt` bytes, the reserved nonce, the
`BlockchainTransaction` and the public token's hash and transaction association in one durable
operator transaction. Only the original operation's receipt, with a factory `ShareTokenCreated`
event matching identifier, symbol and cap, establishes the contract; an identifier lookup or current
cap cannot substitute for attribution, and an existing factory address without an attributable
local deployment stays pending for operator attribution. Unknown outcomes remain `DEPLOYING`.
Projection checks the current token, submission, company, chain and factory before writing public
state, and a failed asset bridge stays recoverable after the contract is recorded. Both clients
distinguish admission, unsigned holds, signed uncertainty, confirmation awaiting projection and the
projected outcome; a contract address alone does not establish attributed completion.

### Automatic swap approval

After the token projection and asset bridge succeed, a bounded operator transaction commits
`projected_at`, a frozen approval disposition and the exact `recover_swap_approval` job together on
the existing private `TokenDeployment`. The company deployment's consumed approval and applying actor
stay separate from this technical approval. Approval runs asynchronously and never reverses a
successful deployment. It has separate intent, outcome, outgoing operation and transaction fields;
factory deployment evidence is never reused or reset. `check_pending_swap_approvals` selects
admitted pending or executing approvals independently of deployment recovery.

Admission captures the original deployment's chain and signer, the attributed share-token address,
the configured swap target and the exact approval calldata. A missing, malformed or zero swap
address records `not_configured` with no approval authority. Historical projected deployments keep
blank approval fields. Later settings cannot admit, enable or retarget either category
automatically, and before fresh signing the configuration must still match the original intent.

An initial verified `approvedShareTokens` reading at a recorded block can produce
`observed_approved` without a local transaction; its immutable block and time evidence records an
observation, never transaction attribution. A false reading commits an executing decision before
opening the common operation, and from then on current approval state cannot replace the original
transaction's result, including when another observer finishes late. The original successful
receipt and a unique matching `ShareTokenApproved(token, true)` event from the admitted contract
confirm the approval. A retained revert can be projected locally while the provider is unavailable.

The admin **Retry approval** action requires current active staff with token change permission and
a signed form identifying the actor, deployment and exact completed failed claim. The previous
transaction's receipt survives retry; replay acknowledges accepted work without opening another
claim. The app role cannot read or write approval metadata.

### Whitelist changes

The participant's nomination and the company's decision are described in
[participants](../business/participants.md#wallet-nominations-and-company-wallet-approvals).

`WhitelistEntry` is the one identity row per wallet: it either points at a `Wallet` or carries a
bare `address` plus a `label` for an operator-held treasury address, and a database constraint
requires one of the two and makes bare addresses unique. Each company approval is a
`WhitelistApproval` row for that entry and company, mirroring the company registry's expiry.

| Route under `/api/v1/whitelist/` | Operations |
| --- | --- |
| `wallet-nominations/` | Participant: list, detail, `preview/` and create |
| `company-wallet-nominations/` | Company: nomination reads |
| `company-wallet-instructions/` | Company: create, list, detail, `decision-preview/` and `decide/` |
| `company-wallet-targets/` | Company: the retained CONFIRMED or UNCHANGED ADD targets a REMOVE can name |

A participant nominates one own Base wallet, with its genuine retained possession proof, to the exact
company and its current GENERAL eligibility source. Company reads expose that nomination's minimal
facts (address, chain, eligibility source, proof completion time, expiry and readiness), not an
account wallet directory. The private proof, nominee associations and signature bytes remain
operator-only, and historical VERIFIED status is not backfilled into a proof.

A current personal company `admin` appointment satisfies each step; narrower prepare, approve, apply
and read capabilities remain. ADD freezes the nomination, company, address, chain, actual registry,
finite expiry (in the registry's whole seconds, never beyond the captured eligibility expiry) and
exact transaction intent; the factory's `registryOf(acn)` must identify the company's actual deployed
registry. REMOVE freezes a genuine retained CONFIRMED or UNCHANGED ADD journal and its exact company,
registry and address, including historical NULL-source ADDs; deleting the mutable wallet, entry or
approval does not erase that target, and an approval-only historical row with no ADD journal is not
promoted into command history.

Application consumes the retained approval and atomically admits and queues one original
`WhitelistChange`: applied status means admission, not chain approval. The one write is
`setExpiry(address, uint64)`: ADD carries its finite whole-second expiry and REMOVE writes zero. A
database trigger binds the change's company, address, action and expiry into that exact calldata. The
nullable immutable source link preserves old journals without invented actors or approvals. Staff
have no fresh ADD or REMOVE admission; retained private entry reads, sync and export, and original
technical recovery remain. Proposal, consumed approval, change, outgoing operation, claim and
transaction identities stay separate.

The target advisory lock precedes the company and source locks, and a fresh signature rechecks the
captured nomination and proof, GENERAL source and expiry with the company mandates. Permanent source
loss on never-signed work fails the original claim and projects that failure, releasing its target
so a genuine eligibility-loss REMOVE is not stranded behind it; it invents no withdrawal or
wallet-deletion cause.

`pending` and `executing` are unresolved; the nested operation distinguishes PREPARING from SIGNED.
`confirmed` records the original successful transaction; `unchanged` records a real no-transaction
observation; `failed` records a known never-signed failure or revert. The initial observation
compares `expiresAt` with the intended effect; a signed change uses only original receipt
reconciliation, and later membership observations cannot complete it. An unresolved original
serialises its exact chain, registry and address target, so an opposite change waits. Terminal
outcome, local projection and target release commit together, and terminal replay cannot overwrite
newer membership. Projection checks the original company, chain, registry and address and the current
mutable entry association; it cannot recreate a deleted entry or redirect a receipt to its
replacement.

Every registry read the platform makes before acting asks the share class's own registry through the
token's `whitelist()`: issuance execution and order creation both do. Wallet sends of a stablecoin
have no registry to ask and use stored approvals
([EVM transfers](wallet-protocols.md#evm-transfers)).
`GET /api/v1/trading/whitelist/<token>/<address>/status/` answers the registry question for the
share class at contract address `<token>`, for any signed-in user and any address.

What a reader may conclude from an approval's status:

| Status    | What it says |
| --------- | ------------ |
| `pending` | A change for this wallet and company is admitted and unresolved. What the registry holds is unknown, and every platform read treats the wallet as not approved |
| `active`  | The last observation found the registry listing the wallet with this expiry. `is_listed` and the `live` queryset still apply the clock to it |
| `removed` | The last observation found the registry holding zero for it. A removed row is not re-observed by `sync_all_entries` |
| `failed`  | The last change failed or reverted, and is logged at error level. What the registry holds is unknown and every platform read treats the wallet as not approved. `failed` is terminal for that command, so the refresh submits a new one; the thirty-minute `sync_all_entries` re-observes the row and replaces the status with what the registry actually holds |

That is what failing closed means here, and its limits matter. The platform refuses at once, both on
the registry read and on the eligibility checks at order creation and signing. The registry itself
cannot be forced closed while the write is failing, so until a removal lands a direct contract call
can still move shares, and pausing the token is the operator's lever.

### Refreshing an approval

`whitelist.services.refresh` keeps technical eligibility invalidation. It can submit only REMOVE,
using the exact recorded company decision or the actual retained standing, identity or wallet-loss
cause. It does not add or renew approval, authorise a new company instruction, or treat
possession-proof refresh as wallet deletion. A current exact-company GENERAL source can explain why
no removal is needed; absence of a genuine cause is diagnostic, not permission to write.

The sweep reads the actual registry expiry. An already expired address needs no write: its finite
on-chain expiry lapses by itself. An unresolved predecessor is recovered first. Missing receipts,
time or current membership do not release an unresolved signed operation.

Source or request withdrawal, company decision revocation, expiry and evidence purge keep their
applicable decision cause. Account standing or identity loss and wallet verification loss or
deletion keep the actual prior facts and the actor where one exists. Deleting a wallet captures its
company, registry and address targets before the mutable entry and approval cascade and queues their
removal after commit; the `refresh_whitelist_targets` job retries every five minutes until those
removals finish, never substitutes another actor or grants approval, and must stay because the
approval sweep cannot rediscover deleted targets. These attributions remain independent of any new
company instruction.

`refresh_whitelist_approvals` sweeps surviving approvals every five minutes;
`recover_whitelist_changes` recovers original unresolved journals. Unavailable providers, actors,
retained causes or workers stay pending or diagnostic rather than inventing authority. Legacy
accountless treasury entries have no fabricated participant eligibility or possession source; their
retained chain expiry and history is not a treasury-admission policy. Normal intervals do not
guarantee a removal during a provider outage or an unresolved chain outcome.

### Capital increases

What the company supplies and the cap rule are in
[issuing shares](../business/issuing-shares.md#raising-the-authorised-shares).

`/api/v1/tokens/register-capital-increases/` offers preparation, list, detail, decision preview,
decision and the retained authority file (`file`); both clients use the same exact preview and
confirmed decision. Preparation retains the exact authority copy and immutable before-cap, delta and
target against one existing capital request in `UNDER_REVIEW`; it refuses non-increasing or
incoherent terms. Approval records the decision and admits no journal or job. Application consumes
the exact personal approval and commits the original `CapitalIncreaseExecution`, public `executing`
state and durable job together before signing or broadcast. Read-only chain and captured-cap
preflight runs before the decision transaction, outside its locks. Rejection keeps its reason and
rejects the original request in the same command. Fresh owner or staff creation, submission, review
and admission are retired; original private histories remain readable.

The execution calls `setAuthorizedShares(new_authorized_total)` and mints nothing. Its immutable
intent keeps the original token, company, actor, chain, signer, contract, approved target and prior
recorded cap. PostgreSQL guards freeze public identity and non-draft terms, and prevent token cap or
identity edits while the request is executing; pause and unpause remain available. The per-token
in-flight constraint is the hold; network reads, signing preparation and broadcast do not hold that
token lock. Failing a never-signed request after revocation or expiry of its consumed approval
releases the class slot while keeping the intent and journal.

Recovery keeps the exact original terminal receipt before verifying the cap event. Only a matching
`AuthorizedSharesUpdated(oldAmount, newAmount)` event from that contract, in the original canonical
successful receipt, permits atomic completion and cap projection; replay cannot lower a later cap,
and a matching current cap alone never establishes execution. Missing receipts or events stay
unresolved. Contradictory cap observations keep immutable private attribution evidence and the
public hold. A delayed preparer assists a peer's already signed transaction instead of treating its
cap change as unknown. An unavailable read or a changed current cap never justifies constructing a
different retry. The clients show approval, admitted work, the original transaction's status and the
projected result separately.

An unsigned failure or confirmed revert permits **Retry Execute** on the exact failed claim. It needs
`change_capitalincreaserequest` permission and a signed confirmation binding request, dispatch, actor
and claim, and supplies no new company decision; retry admission reacquires the public slot and
records that claim before enqueueing. Already signed recovery needs no renewed human authority. A
genuine superseded predecessor keeps its original figures and attribution; a known failed request
overtaken by a later cap can also retire, an unresolved signed request cannot. New and retry
admission check legacy request states and unexplained same-contract capital transactions, including
orphaned and hashless records; only exact hashes from the admitted operations' signed attempts are
exempt. This conservative fence cannot establish absent historical authority or drain an external
same-key writer.

### Share issuances

What the company does for a
[chain grant](../business/issuing-shares.md#non-paid-chain-grants) or a
[paid issue](../business/issuing-shares.md#company-paid-issues) is in issuing shares.

| Route under `/api/v1/tokens/` | Operations |
| --- | --- |
| `register-issues/` | create, list, detail, `decision-preview`, `decide`, and the retained authority (`file`), terms (`terms-file`) and acceptance (`acceptance-file`) files |
| `register-paid-issues/` | create, list, detail, authority `file`, `decision-preview`, `decide`, and `ready-subscriptions` |

A non-paid chain grant needs a genuine CHAIN opening, an exact same-company member link, the
participant's current nomination and a finite company ADD that the preparation names explicitly;
matching an address alone does not identify that approval. Its review identifies the company, class,
member, nomination, original wallet approval, quantity, documentary fingerprints, opening and
transaction intent; private account, profile, user and proof identifiers stay internal. Preparation
retains authority, terms and any required acceptance evidence and freezes one genuine
`ShareIssuanceRequest` in its existing `UNDER_REVIEW` state without reserving shares; it creates no
paid subscription or receipt. An import-origin book, including an imported zero book after
deployment, cannot use this route.

A paid issue works the same way over an existing subscription recorded as PAID. Preparation captures
the subscription, class, recipient, whole-share quantity, price, payment dates and references, money
held and refund facts; partial receipts and scale-back quantities stay distinct from the original
requested quantity, and a refund owed from a recorded refund. `ready-subscriptions` is an
unpaginated selector for a current `admin` or `prepare` appointee in the exact company and class;
approvers, appliers and register readers inspect existing proposals without it or the wider
financial ledger. Paid fulfilment does not reapply participant eligibility, nomination or grant
requirements.

For both, preparation and approval create no execution effect. Application consumes the exact
approval and atomically makes the request approved and admits its original execution:
`tokens.services.issuance_execution` binds the applied `RegisterInstruction` ISSUE, the consumed
approval, the request (and for a paid issue the original subscription), a private
`ShareIssuanceExecution` and the exact job, reserving the allocation once. Positive whole-share, cap,
reservation and offering headroom checks remain; issued supply, holdings, the cap and reservations
are separate quantities. Fresh staff paid admission is retired; historical accepted commands keep
their original actor and source. App connections cannot read or write private commands, and public
company reads keep their existing shape. Current exact-company register-read capability controls
proposals and retained files; class metadata access exposes no private legacy owner history,
participant wallet or eligibility directory, and company administration grants no access to the
financial ledger. Private account, profile, wallet-lock and reserved dispatch identifiers stay
internal. The request reservation, the actual mint, finality, `ALLOTTED` status, the wallet holding
and the register entry remain separately visible outcomes.

Admission leaves the public request approved and the private command queued. A refund that wins
before the worker's claim rejects the request and keeps a cancelled command in the same transaction;
delayed jobs return that cancellation without opening an operation or creating a public issuance.
The worker commits executing state and its stamped public `ShareIssuance`, keyed
`issuance-request:<uuid>`, before opening the outgoing operation. From that claim onward,
uncertainty blocks refunds. A definite unsigned failure or policy-final original revert permits
refund cancellation or **Retry Execute** on that exact failed claim. Before a fresh signature the
company mandates, member, nomination, finite wallet approval, evidence and intent are rechecked, and
execution checks whitelist membership, the cap and pause state; a known refusal becomes a definite
unsigned failure with a safe explanation.

PostgreSQL guards freeze approved terms, dispatch identity, subscription linkage, payment and share
quantities; recorded refunds cannot be reduced or undone, and token identity stays fixed while new
work can execute. Retained NULL-source paid commands keep their outgoing, token, subscription,
request and private-command suffix.

Recovery keeps the first receipt before checking finality. A transaction marked confirmed or
reverted is evidence of that first inclusion; the issuance stays executing until the
`WALLET_CHAIN_FINALITY_POLICIES` rule for its original network is satisfied, using the same
canonical-block evidence reader as swap settlement. Missing or invalid policy, unavailable evidence,
a changing head or an orphaned receipt leaves the command and its refund hold pending. Before
completion, recovery re-reads the original transaction and verifies the finalised inclusion, sender,
contract and, for success, one matching zero-address `Transfer` for the approved recipient and
amount. A same-outcome reinclusion may complete at its newly verified block: the public transaction
and issuance record that final inclusion while the outgoing journal keeps the first. A changed
success or revert outcome is held for operator attribution.

The locked projection rechecks the original claim, attempt and current finality policy. Private
completion, public issuance, request and subscription allotment commit atomically; terminal replay
preserves that outcome, and a holding refresh uses the admitted contract address.
`check_executing_issuance_requests` recovers bounded batches every five minutes. Completion calls
`record_completed_effects` in the same transaction, and register recording may keep the effect
waiting for its opening, wallet link, attribution or applied instruction
([the register](../business/register.md#who-runs-what)). A grant records its member's ISSUE
once, after the outcome transaction, through the existing wallet-to-member LINK; no member is
inferred from names or addresses. An executed but unentered allocation stays reserved until that
entry exists, because chain completion alone does not establish register recording, and actual
register entry dates stay distinct from subscription allotment dates.

An admitted paid subscription keeps its unique execution and immutable request binding. Permanent
loss of the consumed appointment before any signature keeps that history and the paid money; it does
not renew authority, bind a second request or refund automatically. An actual cancellation still
needs its refund or cancellation facts. Financial receipt and refund producers remain separate, and
new payment mechanics are deferred under [#869](https://github.com/Ledova/ledova/issues/869).

### Pause and unpause

The company decision is in
[issuing shares](../business/issuing-shares.md#pausing-and-unpausing-a-class).

`/api/v1/tokens/register-pause-changes/` is a six-operation family: preparation, list, detail,
authority `file`, decision preview and decision. A current personal company appointment prepares a
strict boolean target state, reason, authority reference and private AUTHORITY evidence; the
proposal freezes the company, class, actor, appointment, configured contract, signer, exact intent
and a copied evidence fingerprint and snapshot. Preparation and approval create no `PauseChange`,
job, signature, nonce or state change. Application consumes the exact approval and current applier
authority and admits one source-bound PENDING `PauseChange` and its durable job atomically under the
original proposal UUID. An unresolved target refuses application before the proposal is consumed;
identical replay recovers the original result and changed terms conflict. Ownership and staff
permissions provide no company mandate. Existing owner-row POST replay and private GET recovery keep
their original account, actor, company, class, UUID and direction; a never-admitted UUID gets an
explicit fresh-admission refusal.

A verified initial boolean at a recorded block may produce `observed`, with its block number, block
hash and observation time and no outgoing operation, signature or nonce. The decision is serialised
against an executing peer. Failing a never-signed original after revocation or expiry of its consumed
approval releases its target slot; definite preparation failures keep their technical meaning.
`confirmed` needs the original canonical successful receipt from the admitted contract and sender
with a unique matching `Paused` or `Unpaused` event, and completed projection; no finality policy is
applied. Current chain state cannot replace that transaction's outcome, and missing or duplicate
events stay unresolved.

The incomplete command reserves its chain and contract, including a confirmed or observed outcome
awaiting public projection. Company projection locks target, company, class, source and journal on
one operator alias before updating the public state. A retained NULL-source pause projects through
its original owner path, and no operator fallback supplies that write. The API and clients show the
requested state and the genuine observation or original receipt separately from current class state:
approval or admitted work does not mean transfers have stopped or resumed.

### Mint requests

The asset mint page, yield-token mint page and request execution page all use
`tokens.services.mint_service`. Minting an active stablecoin on Base can precede enabling that asset
for settlement. Unsupported receiving chains have no mint action, and direct mint-page requests are
refused before creating a request. A mint form keeps a submission UUID across a repeated POST;
reusing it with different terms or another actor is refused, and a fresh form is a deliberate new
mint. The service checks current active staff and the entry point's model permission before admitting
work. Admission commits the token, deployment, chain, signer, recipient, raw amount, calldata and
executing actor before opening an outgoing operation.

Every new request has a dispatch UUID, and its outgoing key includes both request and dispatch UUIDs.
PostgreSQL freezes admitted terms and the operation association, requires a confirmed matching
operation and original transaction projection for completion, and refuses deletion of admitted
requests. Execution checks current deployment eligibility, signer configuration and on-chain minter
permission before signing. Signed recovery uses the recorded intent after configuration changes; it
never switches the recorded signer or deployment.

Once a settlement-asset mint is executed, by its execution or by recovery, the AUDY holding of each
verified Base wallet registered at the recipient address is written from `balanceOf` after the mint
has committed; a holding that cannot be written is logged and leaves the mint executed. A yield-token
mint writes none.

A missing receipt or lost send acknowledgement leaves the request **Outcome unresolved**; use
**Recover** on the same request. `recover_mint_requests` selects at most 100 admitted unresolved
requests, oldest update first, with operator authority. It does not admit pending requests or
restart terminal attempts. A recorded pre-signing failure or revert permits an explicit **Retry**
tied to the claim shown on that form; replaying an old form cannot authorise a later attempt.
Recovery updates the transaction projection from its operation; if a worker stops before that
projection, the sweep repairs it without provider access or another signed attempt. **Executed**
means a canonical successful receipt was observed; mints apply no finality policy.

### NAV updates

The staff YieldToken **Update NAV** form requires `tokens.change_yieldtoken`. Its signed form
identifier belongs to that staff member and token: retrying it returns its original outcome, changed
values, mode or actor are refused, and **Start a new NAV update** opens a fresh form for deliberate
new work. Metadata edits save only the edited fields, so an older form cannot overwrite a NAV that
completed while the form was open.

Local-only updates need no chain configuration and commit the NAV audit row, token valuation,
original same-symbol asset and daily snapshot together. Chain updates first commit a private
`NAVUpdate` and its exact recovery job, then sign through the outgoing journal after checking current
staff permission, the original target, signer and contract units. The original successful receipt
must contain exactly one `NAVUpdated` event from the admitted contract with the admitted new NAV and
reserve value; a matching current chain value is insufficient, because even an equal-value update
changes the contract timestamp. An unresolved chain update reserves the token and contract and
refuses every competing submission, including local-only changes, until projection completes; the
exact job and `check_pending_nav_updates` recover it, and the NAV audit page shows its status,
transaction hash and completion. Scheduled NAV price sync locks and rereads the token before locking
the asset, so a captured old quote cannot overwrite a newer completed NAV. Historical NAV rows stay
outside this adapter's admission and recovery.

## Wallets

Possession proof, a participant's eligibility and a company's registry approval are separate facts:
the business rule is in
[participants](../business/participants.md#wallet-nominations-and-company-wallet-approvals).
No company appointment or signing preference grants control of a participant's key.

### Wallet ownership and signing

`VERIFIED` means someone held the private key, not that a hardware device held it. The server issues
a plain-text challenge naming the address, a timestamp and a nonce, and on submission recovers the
signer and compares it with the stored address. `complete_wallet_verification`
(`backend/wallets/services/verification.py`) bounds that three ways: the row is locked for the
read-modify-write, the challenge is refused outside `WALLET_VERIFICATION_CHALLENGE_MINUTES` (five
minutes, the exact boundary included) of its stored issue time, and it is cleared on success so a
signature cannot be replayed; requesting another challenge replaces the nonce and restarts the
window. Success retains the possession proof that nominations require. Master fingerprint,
derivation path and xpub appear nowhere in that path, so a Keystone signature and a script's are
indistinguishable to it. `signing_preference` is self-declared, client-writable and read by no
backend authorisation decision.

The identity check has two branches. `verify_wallet_signature` (`backend/wallets/services/wallets.py`)
dispatches on chain: ETH, ETHEREUM and BASE take `encode_defunct`, `recover_message` and a lowercase
compare, standard EIP-191; BTC and BITCOIN take a base64 compact signature through
`bmt.verify_message`, re-derive the address from the recovered public key by the signature's header
byte, and compare case-sensitively. That branch also refuses any address whose prefix does not match
`BITCOIN_NETWORK` (`m`, `n`, `2` or `tb1` for `test`, `bcrt1` for `regtest`, nothing for any other
value), and `parse_bitcoin_network` in `backend/ledova_backend/chain_safety.py` refuses to start the
process unless `BITCOIN_NETWORK` is `test` or `regtest`.

Two client signing paths exist, and both are legitimate. The Keystone path wraps the challenge as a
UR/CBOR `eth-sign-request`, renders a QR, and reads an `eth-signature` UR back through the camera;
the dashboard's seed-phrase path derives the key in the browser with `localSigner` and never stores
or transmits the phrase. Both clients' `localSigner` modules are built on the shared
`createLocalSigner` (`packages/shared/src/utils/local-signer.ts`), which holds the key handling and
EVM signing; each passes it its own bundled `ethers` and `ethereum-cryptography`, and mobile adds
Bitcoin message signing on the same key handling. The derived private key is wiped in a `finally`,
while the BIP-39 seed and master key are wiped only on `deriveKey`'s normal and guarded exits, so a
throw from `masterKey.derive(derivationPath)` leaves them unzeroed; that throw is reachable because
`derivation_path` is a free-text `CharField` with no format validation.

The dashboard path proves ownership locally before it spends anything. `deriveAddress` runs first
and is compared case-insensitively with the wallet's address, so a wrong phrase is refused in the
browser, not by the server after a round trip. The default path is `m/44'/60'/0'/0/0`, overridden by
a wallet's own `derivation_path`. It is offered only on EVM chains, because the dashboard bundle has
no Bitcoin software signer.

Mobile uses the same endpoint by a different path. It keeps the phrase in the device keychain under
the gated options in [mobile secret storage](mobile.md#secret-storage),
and `autoVerify` reads it back under the same options, so every read raises the OS prompt rather than
only the write at setup. It signs Bitcoin as well as EVM. It asks for no in-app confirmation, gating
only on a self-declared `signingPreference` and a set `derivationPath` and `masterFingerprint`, and
it runs no local `deriveAddress` comparison, so a mismatched row is refused by the server rather than
on the device.

JavaScript and native probes exercise encoding, decoding and scanner boundaries; they do not
establish the combined camera and firmware journey, which stays a
[pre-release device check](mobile-releases.md#pre-release-device-checks).

Reference: `backend/wallets/services/verification.py`,
`dashboard/src/pages/wallets/hooks/useWalletVerification.ts`,
`packages/shared/src/utils/local-signer.ts`, `dashboard/src/utils/softwareWallet/localSigner.ts`,
`mobile/src/utils/softwareWallet/localSigner.ts`, `mobile/src/screens/wallets/useWalletVerification.ts`,
`mobile/src/services/secureKeyStorage.ts`.

### Network identity

Wallet identity is `(account, network, address)`. An account can register the same EVM address on
Ethereum and Base, each with its own UUID, verification, holdings and transactions. EVM address case
variants are the same identity within that account and network; Bitcoin addresses keep their case.

Wallet and transaction address filters require an explicit `chain`. A transaction query may instead
supply a visible `wallet` UUID, from which the network is derived. Queries without an address filter
can still list several networks. Chain filters accept the supported network names and aliases.

Share-token and whitelist operations use the application's Base registry network;
`receiving_wallet_chain` selects the payment network and does not retarget that registry.
Address-based registry identity, issuance holdings and admin wallet selection therefore use Base
wallets, and several accounts with the same Base address remain ambiguous. A company needs its
selected operator wallet, or a verified owner wallet, on the requested network; Ethereum wallets do
not stand in for Base wallets. Historical whitelist rows linked to another network stay visible to
operations for review and are excluded from registry address resolution; their wallet ownership is
not rewritten automatically.

## Assets and valuation

### Asset identity and allowlisting

Asset identity is `(chain, contract_address)` through `AssetChainDeployment`. A contract the
allowlist does not know is recorded as an unverified `Asset` under a symbol no other row owns (the
declared symbol, or the symbol plus a growing hex prefix of the contract address), compared
case-insensitively.

- Unverified rows are invisible to customers: the asset list, wallet holdings, transactions, market
  values and price sync all filter on `is_verified`. A quarantined row is never priced, and its
  transaction is kept for audit without opening a `Holding`.
- Allowlist a token with the asset admin's **Mark selected assets as verified (allowlist a
  quarantined token)** action.
- Switch a contract off by deactivating its chain deployment. Transfers for it are then skipped and
  logged, never booked.
- The same address on another chain is a different contract and gets its own unverified row. Add a
  second chain's deployment to a verified row by hand in the admin.

### A share holding names its class

A share class becomes an asset when it is deployed (`bridge_share_asset`), named after the company
and the class, with the class's Base contract as its deployment. That contract is the only link
between the two. So `GET /api/wallets/{uuid}/holdings/` resolves each row's `shareClass` (`uuid`,
`name`, `symbol` and `companyName`) as it reads: from the asset's Base contract to the class deployed
there, inside the holdings query and under the caller's policies (`with_share_class` in
`backend/wallets/querysets/share_class.py`). The class lookups use one SQL statement; the route's
deployment and yield reads remain separate. An absent Base deployment, unmatched contract or class
hidden by those policies leaves `shareClass` null and preserves the holding. An investor reads
another company's class only while it is on the market, so a paused class keeps its row with
`shareClass` null and the asset's combined name stands in; a company owner can still read the names
of its own paused class under the owner policy. Class and company renames appear on the next read
without rewriting the asset's combined name ([decision](../decisions.md#the-signed-in-app)).

Asset symbols are unique, so when two companies' classes share a symbol the second asset bridged is
stored as the symbol followed by its company's ACN (`ORD.123456782`), or by part of its contract
address when that is taken too. The stored symbol stays the asset's identity: the API's
`assetSymbol`, the asset admin, the staff transactions list's asset filter and the account export
carry it. Each Activity row (`GET /api/transactions/`) resolves the same `shareClass` the same way,
and wherever a person reads a class it reads by its own symbol with its company: Activity amounts
(`40 ORD`, beside the class's combined name), transaction notices ("100 ORD (Example Pty Ltd)") and
the staff lists of holdings and transactions ("ORD (Example Pty Ltd)"). Crypto keeps its asset
symbol, and so does a class the reader cannot see, such as another company's paused class, which
shows its stored symbol as Holdings shows its combined name.

Neither client's Send picker offers shares: it filters on the asset type, so a paused class with no
readable class details stays excluded. Crypto and payment tokens remain available, and the backend
keeps refusing direct share transfers
([transfer lifecycle](wallet-protocols.md#transfer-lifecycle)).

### Holdings written from the chain

A holding is written from the chain (`balanceOf`) wherever the platform moves shares or its
settlement asset: the recipient's share holding when an issuance completes, the recipient's AUDY
holding when a deposit's mint executes (each verified Base wallet registered at that address), and
the buyer's and the seller's share and AUDY holdings when a trade settles. Each write runs after the
work that moved the tokens has committed, so a node that fails at that moment leaves the issuance,
mint or trade complete and the holding unwritten, and none of these writes creates a holding for a
balance of zero.

The wallet sync (`sync_all_wallets` hourly, and **Sync balances**) refreshes every verified holding
and then looks for what the wallet holds without one: the share classes it can hold and the
operator's settlement asset. A share contract credits only an address on its company's registry, so
the sync reads the deployed classes of the companies that have approved the wallet, whether or not
that approval is still live. The settlement asset, which anyone can hold, it reads when the operator
settles in exactly one verified asset and the wallet is on the chain the operator settles on. It
writes each positive balance it finds, which recovers a holding a write above could not make and
tokens sent to the wallet outside the platform. A balance it cannot read is skipped without failing
the sync. Pending transfers can cap a refresh
([balance repair](wallet-protocols.md#balance-repair)).

### Valuation sources

Portfolio values and new asset snapshots use USD. Asset price writes record `market` for provider or
manual quotes, `nav` for NAV updates, or `par` for the configured par reference. AUDY uses one AUD
per token divided by the stored USD/AUD exchange rate; seed-only does not create an FX quote. Sync
exchange rates before refreshing asset prices. Without a positive finite rate, a new AUDY holding
stays unpriced. A later provider outage preserves the last valid cached quote, as it does for market
prices; this is not live pricing. Manual quotes in another currency also need a stored conversion
rate. A cached price whose source is unknown is excluded from current valuations until a producer
refresh or the admin **Update prices for selected assets** action records it; direct price, currency
and source fields are read-only in the admin.

### Transaction values and monitoring windows

Each wallet transaction, imported by sync or sent from the app, records its USD and AUD values when
it is recorded (`transaction_values` in `backend/assets/services/valuation.py`). The USD value is the
amount at the asset's USD price at the block time, and the AUD value is that converted at the USD/AUD
rate stored at that moment; an asset with an AUD par, AUDY, is valued at par whatever its USD price.
A transfer sent from the app is recorded before it is mined, so it is valued when it is sent, and
wallet sync later finds the hash already recorded and skips it, so it counts once.

`sync_exchange_rates` refreshes the rate every 10 minutes and keeps no rate history. The
per-transaction monitoring rules check only transactions whose block time is less than an hour old
(`TRANSACTION_MONITORING_WINDOW_HOURS`), or that are not mined yet, so for them the rate is the one
in effect when they happened. History imported later, such as a newly verified wallet's past
transfers, is converted at the rate stored when it is imported. A transaction with no USD price, or
recorded while no rate was stored, has no AUD value, and no amount rule counts it. Rule windows run
on when a transaction happened (its block time or, for a transfer not mined yet, when it was
recorded), so imported history counts when it happened, not as a burst of recent activity.

A customer is new for `NEW_CUSTOMER_DAYS` after their account first became active, and an account
with no activation date counts as new. The account records that moment once, whichever path
activates it, and the `users_keep_first_activation` trigger keeps the earliest date through
suspension and reactivation; it can still move earlier, which is how the demo seed dates its testers
from their identity decision. The rules, their thresholds and what they mean for an account are in
[participants](../business/participants.md#transaction-monitoring).

### Portfolio history

There is none. A `Holding` carries only its current quantity: the hourly sync, history imports and
transfer confirmation rewrite that quantity and keep no dated series, and no valuation is computed
for a past date. No snapshot route, holding snapshot or value-series service exists; the portfolios
API only groups wallets. Dashboard and mobile Holdings pages show share quantities by company and
class instead of a portfolio chart.
